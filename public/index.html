Memoirs of a Wanderer — the gloomcore reel

Shot in the
last blue light.

Four photographs from the North Coast, made in low light and left almost untouched. Scroll slowly. Let the weather do the talking.

Scroll ↓
Plate I — The Lily Pond

Some ducks I happened to come across

The water had gone that deep evening blue a camera reads better than the eye. A koi drifted like a smudge of rust beneath the surface. Then one of the geese opened its wings — white banners against the dark — and the whole pond held its breath. No filter was harmed in the making of this picture. The dusk did the work. I simply happened to be standing there, which is most of what wandering is.

Plate II — Monochrome

Signal & steeple

Two Norfolk pines stand shoulder to shoulder with a cell mast, as if the tower were trying, and failing, to pass for a tree. There is a whole essay in that quiet impersonation — nature and infrastructure keeping the same evening watch.

Plate III — Passage

The long way out

A slatted boardwalk threads across the tidal flat and vanishes into a wall of green. At this hour the timber glows faintly, the only warm thing for a mile. You walk it because the alternative is turning back, and the light is nearly gone.

Plate IV — Tideline

Static tide

The fog swallowed the coastline whole, leaving one distant tower block as the only evidence of a city. The surf came in bright and cold and grainy, like film left too long in the drawer. A grey day that photographed like a memory.

Quiet Bloom

Quiet Bloom

Where still waters cradle forgotten blooms, darkness gives way to quiet beauty.
